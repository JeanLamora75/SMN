# Sprint page: follow the complete life of a sprint

The **sprint page** is your team's working record. It brings together goals, ceremonies, capacity, indicators, register items, trends and the sprint review. Unlike the sprint list, which helps you **find and plan cycles**, this page helps you **manage and understand one specific sprint**.

> **Camille's tip:** revisit the sprint page throughout the iteration, not just at closure. Good records save you from reconstructing the team's history at the end.

## 1. Start with the header

The dark header displays the **sprint name**, **team and product**, **dates** and **time zone**. Check these before changing anything.

With suitable permissions, you may edit the name and, while the sprint is not closed, its dates. Changing dates can affect planning, ceremonies and historical interpretation.

Header actions may include **Generate AI brief**, **Generate report** and the current **sprint status**. **Finished** and **Closed** are different states: closing restricts further changes and may preserve historical snapshots.

## 2. Objectives: define the expected result

The **Objectives** tab explains **why** the sprint matters, beyond a list of tasks.

1. Review existing sprint objectives.
2. Select **Add objective** when authorized.
3. Write a measurable outcome that describes user or business value.
4. Review the objective when the sprint ends.

For example, “Customers can view incidents without support” is clearer than “Implement incident screen”.

For some finished sprints, authorized users may see **Enter partial historical review**, used to complete missing historical information.

## 3. Ceremonies: keep meetings and outcomes together

This tab displays sprint meetings, potentially including **Planning, Daily Scrum, Review and Retrospective**.

Ceremonies can be **generated when the sprint is created**, from templates configured in Settings. The tab shows saved occurrences; editing a template later does not necessarily rewrite those already created.

Depending on permissions and sprint status, you can review meeting times, open a meeting, update supported details, add suitable meetings or historical records, and verify the result in the [Calendar](/app/team-calendar).

> **Tip:** record the outcome of important meetings, not only their planned times.

## 4. Capacity: understand real team availability

Capacity is not simply headcount multiplied by sprint days. SMN considers relevant **assignments, working calendars, Agile roles and absences**.

The tab can show **total person-day presence**, **development capacity**, role-specific **Product Owner / Scrum Master** capacity, member count and a member table with function, contribution, presence, absences and development capacity.

It also reports when information is missing or ambiguous. Use **Manage members** or Members to review assignments, work patterns and absences.

> **Remember:** missing capacity is not zero. A closed sprint may show historical snapshots while an open sprint can show projected values.

## 5. Indicators: explain sprint measurements

**Manual indicators** are values recorded for the sprint by authorized users, possibly using data from Tickets / CSV. **Computed indicators** follow formulas defined in **Settings → Indicators**.

Team selections and display preferences determine which indicators appear in the cycle detail. Authorized users can enter manual values and review calculated output in this tab.

Always check the indicator's unit, sources and cycle before presenting it. A missing value may indicate missing source data rather than a zero result.

## 6. Registers: preserve the context behind results

Review sprint **risks, impediments and dependencies**, along with relevant **decisions, actions and highlights** where supported.

Use available type filters, open records to inspect their status and details, and update authorized items while the sprint is not closed. The [Registers](/app/admin/registries) module also allows linking related records.

> **Tip:** keep resolved impediments in the record. They show what the team overcame.

## 7. Trends: compare several cycles

The **Trends** tab displays indicators over multiple sprints where data and permissions allow it.

Check measurement units and indicator definitions before comparing periods. Changes may be explained by staffing, absences, scope or other events. An empty chart can indicate missing historical data, unselected indicators or restricted access.

> **Tip:** use a trend to start a discussion, not to deliver an automatic judgement about team performance.

## 8. Review: tell the sprint's story

The **Review** tab has distinct spaces for:

**Manual review** — A human-authored summary of achievements, differences, challenges and next steps.

**AI review** — A place to store or edit an AI-related summary. The ability to edit it manually does not mean the AI generated it automatically.

When permitted, use the edit control to update content. SMN can display version, author and date. Closed sprints have editing restrictions and may use closure history.

> **Suggested structure:** “Intended goal → Observed results → Differences → Decisions and next actions”.

## 9. Prepare closure and reporting

Before closing or generating a report, review all seven tabs:

1. Are **objectives** and results clear?
2. Do **ceremonies** include meaningful outcomes?
3. Does **capacity** reflect member availability?
4. Are **indicators** complete and understood?
5. Are important **register records** up to date?
6. Are **trends** comparable?
7. Does the **review** distinguish facts from interpretation?

The [Reports](/app/reports) module can reuse supported sprint data in compatible Word and PowerPoint templates.

## 10. Troubleshooting

- **Missing objectives:** none may have been recorded yet.
- **Missing ceremonies:** check templates and generated occurrences.
- **Unknown capacity:** review assignments, calendars, roles and absences.
- **Missing indicator:** check team selection and display preferences.
- **Missing register record:** check filters and cycle associations.
- **Unavailable trend:** check historical measures and permissions.
- **Editing blocked:** check your permissions and whether the sprint is **Closed**.

## Your final check

Can you explain **what the team intended, what it achieved, what capacity was available, what events influenced the results and what to improve next**?

If those answers are in the seven tabs, your sprint page is a useful team history and planning resource.
